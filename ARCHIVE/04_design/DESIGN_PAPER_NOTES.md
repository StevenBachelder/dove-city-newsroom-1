# Design is the beginning, middle and end
### Working notes toward a paper on the power and necessity of design in educational games
*Living document — append episodes as they occur. Started July 2026, City Newsroom project.*

**Thesis.** In experience-based learning, design is not the packaging of content but the medium of it: the learner's journey is authored in mechanics, fiction, feedback and friction before a single "learning objective" is met. Every episode below is a case where a design decision *was* the pedagogy.

**Three uses.** (1) *Paper spine* — evidence base for publication. (2) *Mentoring scaffold* — tacit design knowledge made articulate: each principle + live episode is a critique reference a junior designer can be pointed to, and the checklist below turns them into review questions. (3) *Designer’s whetstone* — a comprehensive reference built from one’s own process, for staying sharp against one’s own standards.

**Method note.** This is design-based research (DBR) in the wild: iterative design ↔ classroom/playtest feedback, with each cycle documented. The game's research log (?research=1) and pre/post instruments give the quantitative rail; this document is the qualitative rail.

## Principle → Episode → Learning consequence

**1. Interface as fiction (suspension of disbelief).**
Episode: playtesters pinned images just to look at them; tap-to-enlarge was added; a tester described the result as "it does what you think it should, so you wander freely without questioning the fiction."
Consequence: frame acceptance is a measurable precondition for situated learning; friction reports are curriculum signals. (Cross-ref: GEE_ALIGNMENT addendum.)

**2. Consequence architecture: safe risk with real stakes.**
Episode: the one-mistake-per-level standard, level restarts, and the door (polite firing after four failures).
Consequence: bravery becomes rational; failure becomes named diagnosis (the level report tells you which technique won). The ending where Sarah shows you the door and the ending where your Master badge "hangs proudly by the door" are the same door — designed consequence carries the game's moral arithmetic.

**3. Reward as explanation: the key-evidence system.**
Episode: smoking-gun sources earn elevated points, a golden ceremony, and a context note that states *why this source falsifies the claim* — the note replaced the generic praise line in the evidence review.
Consequence: the reward channel and the explanation channel are one channel. Players are paid in understanding.

**4. Inspection free, judgment scored.**
Episode: enlarging media costs nothing; pinning (selection, 3 max) is the scored act.
Consequence: the economy mirrors epistemics — looking should be cheap, committing should be deliberate. The 3-pin limit makes *selection* the skill.

**5. Coherence is pedagogy; incoherence is a teaching defect.**
Episode: verdict texts that still described old premises (military exercise vs. Red Bull ad; pets vs. cats; generic vs. doctored-stock water) were treated as bugs of the same severity as broken code, and repaired in dedicated "coherence passes."
Consequence: learners generalize from *everything* on screen; a mismatched explanation teaches the mismatch.

**6. Constraint-driven design: limitations recast as content.**
Episode: missing media (LFS stubs) forced the same doctored photo into two result slots — which became the astroturf lesson ("the same picture everywhere"); a missing dark-smoke variant produced the identical-timestamp pair mechanic.
Consequence: constraints, honestly engaged, generate authentic patterns of manipulation better than invention does.

**7. The world teaches across cases (serialized fiction).**
Episode: BAD DOG's staged death (flag the hoax) is followed by his true exposure (publish the truth) — belief and dismissal each punished once; 4Ms Laboratories signs its own fake PSA in end-credits; ICPAA embodies the imposter sender; a follower-shop ad circles the follower-buying scandal ("the scam advertises itself").
Consequence: transfer rides on narrative continuity; the liar's dividend is *experienced* (a manipulator's noisy reputation shields him) before it is named.

**8. The taxonomy grows from the fiction, not by decree.**
Episode: the Impersonation badge was born because a case rework (ICPAA) made the imposter-sender pattern playable; the crosswalk gap closed itself through design.
Consequence: concepts earn their place by being enacted; a badge is a certificate that a pattern was *met*, not merely listed.

**9. Ceremony consolidates (arrival → energize → inscription).**
Episode: the Master card lands physically atop the collection, sends a wave that re-values every badge (+25→+30, counters re-tallying), then fades into a written citation below the grid.
Consequence: assessment as ritual: the score change is watched, then explained, then inscribed — mastery as a visible transformation of everything previously earned.

**10. Player-led iteration as method.**
Episode: the pin-look-unpin workaround, the mobile foot-cropping report, the overlapping press-card text — each playtester friction became a same-week design change and, where warranted, a documented principle.
Consequence: the feedback loop is the curriculum development process; the designer's job is to keep the loop's latency short.

**11. Score as ecological value system (procedural choreography).**
Episode: the evidence economy was re-tiered so that point values mirror epistemic roles — weak corroboration (+1, quiet), pair evidence (+3, keyed, needing its partner), decisive proof (+5, keyed, case-closing) — with the key ceremony gated to a threshold so celebration tracks warrant. The trigger was a single mismatched caption; the repair reorganized the whole ladder.
Consequence (designer's formulation, preserved): *the score is an ecological core value system in dialogue with the choices and actions of the player inside the magic circle — a procedural choreography between player and the game’s ecological world.* In Gibsonian terms: every element’s affordances (pin, zoom, watch-to-end, compare) are invitations calibrated to epistemic worth, and the score is the ecology’s voice answering each uptake. The player is not graded from outside the world; the world itself responds — which is why the feedback teaches rather than merely ranks. (Cross-refs: Gibson, ecological psychology; Huizinga / Salen & Zimmerman, magic circle; principles 3 and 4 above are special cases of this one.)

Principle: one artifact, three pedagogical channels; production style itself is a teachable pattern.

## As a critique checklist (ask these of any educational game — or of a junior’s build)
1. Where does the interface remind the player it is software — and can that seam be removed? *(Interface as fiction)*
2. Is failure survivable, named, and diagnostic — or merely punished? *(Consequence architecture)*
3. When the game rewards, does it also explain — or does it pay in empty points? *(Reward as explanation)*
4. What is free and what is scored — and does that split mirror how the real skill works? *(Inspection free, judgment scored)*
5. Does every text, image and verdict tell the same story as the mechanics? *(Coherence is pedagogy)*
6. What constraint are you fighting that could become content instead? *(Constraint-driven design)*
7. Do concepts recur across cases so the world itself carries transfer? *(Serialized fiction)*
8. Is every taught concept actually enacted somewhere — or merely listed? *(Taxonomy from fiction)*
9. Where does mastery become visible, witnessed, and inscribed? *(Ceremony consolidates)*
10. What did the last playtester struggle with — and how fast did it change the build? *(Player-led iteration)*
11. Is the score an external grade — or the world’s own ecology responding to the player? *(Ecological value system)*
