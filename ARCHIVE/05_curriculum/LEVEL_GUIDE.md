# City Newsroom — Level & Case Curriculum Guide (v1, working)
*Step 1 of the Five-Step Cycle · started Aug 2026 · feeds: research log, game design, teacher material*

## The Five-Step Cycle (process contract)
1. **Guide (this doc):** each level and each tip assessment described as *method* toward targeted learning outcomes, tied to design principles (ARCHIVE/04_design) and literature (RESEARCH_ALIGNMENT).
2. **Analysis:** rigorous pass over all 15 assessments + structure; recommendations ranked by viability this cycle.
3. **Adjust:** implement accepted recommendations.
3b. **Evidence gate:** where playtest data exists (Uppsala, youth panel), changes are judged against players, not only designer reasoning.
4. **Re-analysis:** repeat step 2 on the adjusted deck.
5. **Guide v2:** conclude; diff against v1 becomes research-log material.

## The three levels — what each achieves

### Level I — "See For Yourself" (Verification)
**Learning outcome:** the core reflex — leave the page; find the original; both errors count.
**Signatures taught:** fabrication (water), manipulation with a repeated frame (Old Town), convergence of independent sources (Downtown), sender identity (library party), the boring true (monkey).
**Design logic:** clear cases, strong tells, the fire twins as contrast pair; failure is named and survivable.

### Level II — "True to Size" (Proportion / Calibration)
**Learning outcome:** two questions, not one — *did it happen, and at what scale?* Kernel-and-shell; emotion ≠ false; dramatic-and-true exists.
**Signatures taught:** kernel-of-truth amplification (gas), emotionally loaded truth (winter storm), reputation as shield (BAD DOG exposé), doctored magnitude, ad economies circling stories.
**Design logic:** every case contains something true; the skill is sizing, not sorting.

### Level III — "Through the Fog" (Judgment under ambiguity)
**Learning outcome:** adversarial sophistication — operations, not accidents; your own tools and footage weaponized; provenance as the last firm ground.
**Signatures taught:** impersonation of institutions (ICPAA), cross-case operations (4Ms / Guns For Hire), the crop as lie (storming), AI at scale, the liar's dividend resisted by archives.
**Design logic:** cases interlock; solutions live partly in *other* cases (transfer is the mechanic).

## Level ceremonies (copy drafts, EN — ×4 after approval)
**Intro pattern:** "Welcome to {level name}. {One-sentence craft statement.}" (In-game copy now uses game-lingo Levels I–III; the shift metaphor is retired.) 
e.g. L2: "Welcome to the Second Shift: True to Size. From here on, almost everything contains some truth — your job is to weigh it, so the story you stamp is the size of the facts."
**Outro pattern (attempt-sensitive):**
- 1st try: "Cleared on the first attempt — not everyone manages that."
- 2nd try: "Cleared in two — a second look is a verifier's oldest tool."
- 3+ tries: "It took a few attempts — some never make it at all. Doing the work paid off."
Then, all: "You've shown {x, y, z}. Next: {next shift name} — {one-line preview}."

## Per-case takeaway template (from the legacy gas text — the four moves)
1. Report only what is **confirmed**. 2. State clearly what is **unknown**. 3. **Cite** the source class that confirms. 4. Name the situation's **stability** ("developing — may change").
Level tuning: L1 emphasizes 1+3; L2 adds 2 (scale unknowns); L3 adds 4 (instability as a finding).

## Case entries — the five questions (template + worked exemplars)
*Template per case: (1) What the player sees · (2) What they must discern · (3) Where the solution lives (context level / transfer) · (4) Which disinformation aspect is revealed · (5) Which pedagogical tools are actualized (affordances put into play).*

### Exemplar A — #10 Toxic Release (L2)
1. A worried parent's post: visible vapor from a factory chimney, an honest question.
2. That the event is real (authorities converge) AND that the scary version is manufactured (identical 06:12 frames, darkened plume); that an official-sounding amplifier ("For Our Public Safety") adds urgency, not information.
3. Solution spans the case: pair comparison (in-image forensics) + authority scale-check (search side). Transfer: sender-name itch learned from ICPAA (L3 backward-echo in review).
4. Kernel-of-truth amplification; pseudo-official sender.
5. Tap-to-enlarge (inspection free), pair keys + notes (reward as explanation), trap note on the amplifier (negative feedback specific), verdict that names the mechanism.

### Exemplar B — #1/#9 The Fire Twins (L1)
1. Two fires an hour apart: a conspiratorial shriek, then a raw eyewitness question.
2. Twin signatures: the fake repeats one frame (the identical hand); the real converges from many (window, river, windshield, cordon). The archive lookalike must not be mistaken for proof of recycling.
3. Solution lives *between* the cases: the second is solved partly by contrast with the first (sequential arrangement as scaffold).
4. Manipulated imagery; premature certainty as sender-tell; the liar's-dividend reflex ("everything is fake") surfaced and corrected.
5. Contrast pairing, register realism, trap note on the archive, convergence rewarded per angle.

### #2 — Extremist attack on the library (L1)
1. A menacing warning of an attack “tonight,” citing chatter in “religious networks,” beside a calm city library.
2. That the threat has no primary source: the scary posts cite each other (securitysource.com sells the fear it reports — its own premium channel is an ad); the library and police report normality. Absence of convergence IS the finding.
3. In the search layer almost entirely — the first case where the answer is “who profits from my fear?” Transfer forward: securitysource.com returns in the monkey case, same technique.
4. Fear entrepreneurship; conspiracy framing (“the government planned it”); the security-ad economy circling threat stories.
5. Funding-sender question (tip guide fold), ad marking, sender continuity, verdict naming the no-primary-source pattern.

### #3 — Water poisoning (L1)
1. A neighbor’s alarming claim that district water is poisoned, with a convincing test-strip photo.
2. That the photo is doctored stock imagery — the erasure blurs where watermarks were removed ARE visible — while every authority (water department, EPA, labs) reports normal seasonal changes.
3. Inside the image (the blur forensic, keyed pair with the clean stock original) + the search layer’s converging officialdom. The panic-ad ring (filters, testing kits) mirrors the library case one tip earlier.
4. Fabricated evidence; manufactured health scare; the scare-commerce ecosystem.
5. Tap-to-enlarge as the magnifying glass, key pair with erasure note (“the erasure marks ARE the manipulation”), ad economy as recurring cast.

### #5 — Escaped Monkey Alert (L1)
1. A charming, slightly absurd street scene: a monkey in rush-hour traffic, warm bystander captions.
2. That absurd-but-true exists: officials confirm the escape; the deliberately silly satellites (skateboarding monkey, “urban wildlife collapse”) must be separated from the confirmable core. A weak corroborating street photo pays +1 — seeing is corroboration, not verification.
3. Search layer (officials + animal control converge) with in-case triage of joke content. Transfer: first meeting with securitysource.com’s “government program” framing and City Frontline’s escalation — signatures that recur for three levels.
4. The boring/delightful true; satire adjacent to news; absurdity ≠ falsity.
5. Graduated evidence values (+1 quiet support), sender continuity seeding, verdict rewarding publication of the odd-but-confirmed.

### #6 — Military vehicles rolling through the city (L2)
1. Grainy night footage of a long armored column; whispers of “what are they hiding?” and imminent martial law.
2. That the clip is a two-year-old Red Bull commercial — the complete film (decisive key, +5) plus the City Film Office’s night-shoot permit close the case; around it, conspiracists (The Fish Lovers) had already pre-written democracy’s fall, and prepper commerce sells the panic.
3. Provenance layer: reverse-video to the original ad, paper trail to the permit. Transfer: the recycled-fiction pattern echoes the fire twins’ archive lesson at higher stakes.
4. Conspiracy scaffolding on out-of-context fiction; footage laundering (ad → “leak”); fear commerce (survival-gear ads).
5. Single-handedly decisive key evidence, date forensics, ad marking, verdict naming how conspiracy pre-narration works.

### #9 — Monster winter storm (L2)
1. A breathless “share before it’s too late!!” storm warning with dramatic sea-wall clips — everything about it smells like clickbait.
2. That it is dramatic AND true: red warning issued, independent cameras agree, crews securing the harbour — while mocking skeptics (“storm panic is fake,” “only cowards hide indoors”) bait the wrongful flag.
3. Search layer: authority + independent convergence; the trap is affective, not evidential. Transfer: the calibration learned in the gas case, now applied to tone instead of magnitude.
4. Emotionally loaded truth (emotion ≠ false); wrongful disbelief and its cost (lost preparation time); bravado skepticism as its own manipulation.
5. Support convergence rewarded, skeptic posts as flag-bait, verdicts pricing the cost of disbelief, level theme (True to Size) enacted.

### #10 — The REAL crowd size (L2)
1. A cropped photo claiming to expose the station’s “lie” about protest attendance — the tip attacks the player’s own newsroom.
2. That the crop comes from City News’ own wide-angle footage: the full frame shows the real crowd; the police report corroborates. The sparse result set is itself the test — few sources, but the right ones.
3. In-house provenance: the newsroom’s own archive is the decisive ground. Transfer backward to “the crop is the lie”; forward to L3’s weaponizing of the desk’s own footage.
4. Out-of-context cropping; institutional trust attack; the liar’s dividend aimed at journalism itself.
5. Own-archive verification, minimal-evidence confidence, recurring fear-commerce sponsor (UrbanSafe) across unrelated scares.

### #11 — New infection spreading among cats (L3)
1. An official-looking health alert from “ICPAA”: a new infection among cats, alarming imagery, slick PSA films.
2. That the sender is the story: ICPAA is a dog-advocacy lobby, not a health authority (the exposé, +5); the alarming photos are a 2021 Asia outbreak recycled; the trio of “sick” cats are different animals; and the PSA films sign their own forgery — 4Ms Laboratories in the end credits, watchable only to the very end.
3. Sender-identity layer + in-media forensics + archive. Transfer: the desk’s own reverse-image-search training film shares the fakes’ production style — the double irony is named in review.
4. Institutional impersonation; astroturfed authority; recycled outbreak imagery; interest-group laundering.
5. Watch-to-the-end keys, exposé as decisive source, sender-name itch, diegetic-film style priming.

### #12 — BAD DOG EXPOSED (L3)
1. An 👀 exposé: the death-faking provocateur caught buying followers — surely just another stunt?
2. That the metrics do not lie even when the artist always does: ×10 followers overnight with engagement collapsing (analytics key), while follower-shop ads circle the story selling the same service. His noisy reputation baits the wrongful flag.
3. Platform-metrics layer + the archive of his past stunts. Transfer: sets up the death hoax two tips later — the duology punishes belief once and dismissal once.
4. Reputation as shield (liar’s dividend resisted); the follower economy advertising itself.
5. Generated-analytics key evidence, ad ring as confession, verdict naming the noisy-reputation mechanism.

### #13 — Star footballer tests positive (L3)
1. A career-ending doping claim, hours old, everywhere at once.
2. That nothing confirms it: every source is insinuation — “sources claim,” escort photos, body-language readings, an old podcast clip — while the only authority states testing is routine and confirms nothing. The uncut clip (+5) shows the “escort” moment in full context.
3. Sourcing-quality discrimination: the case is solved by *grading* sources, not finding a smoking gun. Transfer: Level I’s “both errors count” matured into “unverified is its own verdict.”
4. Rumor cascade; unverifiable insinuation; the sports-gossip engagement economy.
5. Decisive full-context key, source-class tells (“sources claim”), sparse-authority weighting.

### #14 — BAD DOG Found Dead! (L3)
1. BREAKING: the artist found dead — gossip channel exclusive, stretcher video, tributes pouring in.
2. That it is his own production: emergency services confirm no death, the “cover-up” angle is fear-merchandise, and @BadDogStudios posted “Better to burn out than to fade away!?” hours *before* the rumors — the hoax signed in advance. The Case Ledger lets the player re-read his exposé from the previous shift.
3. Cross-case memory is the method: his history, reviewed in the ledger, is the strongest source. Transfer: the duology’s second act — yesterday doubt was wrong; today belief is.
4. Self-staged hoax; celebrity-death economy; manufactured cover-up narratives.
5. Ledger as verification tool, pre-planted-post forensics, official convergence against a single-origin story.

### #15 — MASSIVE forest fire (L3)
1. EVACUATE NOW: apocalyptic fire imagery racing toward the city, deletion-panic urgency.
2. That the image is AI-made (one campfire photo, four different hoaxes — the fact-check), no agency reports any fire — and the same signatures that blamed immigrants for the midsummer bonfires are back, citing their own debunked story as evidence.
3. Provenance + cross-case signature comparison (Ledger): the case completes the compounded-xenophobia thread from case #8.
4. AI fabrication at scale; disinformation built on disinformation; scapegoating as the payload.
5. Trap notes on the echo posts, ledger-enabled signature matching, fact-check convergence, verdict naming the wave-on-wave mechanism.

### Ads as curriculum (a note)
Every case’s sponsored ring is part of the lesson: fear commerce tracks fear (UrbanSafe sells against coups and protests alike; ShieldGuard insures libraries and storms; securitysource.com monetizes every panic it reports). Pinning an ad costs −4 — but *reading* them answers the guide’s question: who wants me afraid, and what are they selling?
