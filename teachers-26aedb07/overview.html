Overview & the rules

What it is

A 20–40 min browser game (no accounts; EN/SV/FI/ET): students are trial applicants at a newsroom attacked yesterday, verifying 15 tips over three levels with Text Search and Reverse Image Search. It teaches lateral reading (versus staying "vertically" on the page), sender evaluation — purpose and funding included — and eight named manipulation techniques; mistakes have institutional consequences, and verification is practiced as a repeatable discipline. Above the single-verdict level, it trains pattern sight: posts as possible building blocks of strategic narratives (flattering, undermining, or deflecting via humor and memes), the reach of generative AI, and the liar's dividend — when everything could be fake, denying genuine evidence gets easier. The game begins where traditional source criticism ends: not just "is it true?" but "why is it here, and who gains?"

Before class — 10 minutes

  • Open the game once yourself; hard-refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R).
  • Any modern browser/device; pairs work well (talking is the exercise).
  • Language chosen on the start screen — can differ within a class.
  • Headphones recommended; mute switch in the top bar.
  • Plan 40–60 min total.

Rules students should hear going in

  • Three levels, five tips each; Level 3 fakes don't look fake.
  • One wrong stamp per level tolerated; a second restarts the level (everything restored).
  • Three do-overs total; a fourth failure and Sarah politely shows them the door. Replay is instant.
  • Both errors count — flagging a TRUE story is also a mistake.
  • Three evidence pins max: choosing the strongest is the skill.
