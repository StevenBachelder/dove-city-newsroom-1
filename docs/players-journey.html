The player's journey

A playthrough is a sequence of cycles — enquiry, discovery, dialogue — repeating at three scales.

Arrival

A boarded window, the smell of paint, and a letter from Sarah, Editor-in-Chief: three levels to prove your ability. Your first tip is already waiting.

Every tip (the small cycle)

Read the whole tip. Notice the sender — who is behind this, with what purpose, funded by whom? — the buzz, and your own reaction. Search laterally; hunt the image's original; weigh what the evidence supports — and what the silence says. Choose three pins. Stamp. The verdict answers immediately: the technique that fooled you (or didn't), by name.

Every level (the middle cycle)

Level cleared: your published edition, readers' letters, Sarah's assessment, and a level report — what you caught, what to study, and the badges secured so far. Then harder tips.

The debrief (the large cycle)

The Badge Case: nine cards, earned ones in colour, missed ones in grey — every back a lesson. The Editor's Score. And the Applicant Report: a press card you keep, listing each badge with the skill it certifies.

And above the single verdict, a pattern eye grows: several fakes pointing the same direction are rarely coincidence — they may be building blocks of one larger story. Ask what story, and who gains.

What transfers isn't the score. It's the reflex: leave the page, find the original, and slow down when a story wants your feelings.
