About the project

About City Newsroom

City Newsroom is a serious game teaching young people to recognise disinformation by doing a verifier's job. It runs in any browser, takes 20–40 minutes, needs no accounts, and speaks English, Swedish, Finnish and Estonian. The city is deliberately generic — this could be your city.

The fiction

City News is a combined TV-and-newspaper house. Yesterday it was attacked. The player joins the tip desk the morning after, sorting truth from manufacture while the glazier fixes the window — Level 2 of the SOURCE trilogy.

The research it stands on

The core mechanic is lateral reading (Wineburg & McGrew); the badge system is technique-based inoculation in the lineage of Bad News (Roozenbeek & van der Linden; Axelsson et al. 2024); the learning design follows Gee and Vygotsky; the threat picture draws on Swedish psychological-defence research. Full mappings live in the project archive.

Beyond true or false

Single posts are rarely random. They follow a psychological and algorithmic logic — emotion and technology reward the extreme — and they can be building blocks of strategic narratives: deliberately constructed stories, repeated across many contexts, that shape how people read the world. Some narratives flatter their sender, some undermine a target, and some deflect — steering attention away from uncomfortable questions with humor, memes or conspiracy theories. The game trains the analytic eye for this: seeing a post as a possible piece of a larger pattern, and asking who benefits from the message.

Generative AI raises the stakes twice over: convincing text, image and audio can now be produced at scale — before Slovakia's 2023 election, an AI-generated audio clip appeared to catch a leading candidate planning to rig the vote — and the liar's dividend follows: when anything could be AI, genuine evidence becomes easier to deny. City Newsroom's answer is the verifier's answer: provenance, originals, and documented context.

Societal resilience against influence stands on three legs: free and scrutinizing media, an educated and informed population, and a society with trust that holds together. This game serves the second leg — a generation that recognizes influence when it appears in their feeds. It begins where traditional source criticism ends: not only is this true?, but why is this here, and who gains?
